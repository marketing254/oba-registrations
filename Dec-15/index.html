About this session

Decide before 2027 whether to grow, fix, or hold back

Join the Ophthalmology Business Academy for a focused discussion on knowing where to grow, fix, or hold back before 2027. This panel is designed for ophthalmology owners, administrators, physicians, and leadership teams looking to align strategy with real capacity and sustainable operations.

The conversation will explore the metrics and signals that matter most before investment or expansion, how to separate healthy growth from avoidable operational strain, and how to decide when to add capacity, change direction, or deliberately pause growth.

This panel is for you if…

  • You are planning practice growth or evaluating whether now is the right time to invest
  • You think demand is rising, but you are not sure whether the bottleneck is clinical, operational, or financial
  • You are trying to determine whether your current model needs fixing before more growth can be successful
  • You want to understand the practical signs that growth is outpacing your team, systems, capacity, or patient experience
  • You need a clear framework for deciding when to add capacity, invest, maintain, or hold back
Panelists

Who's on the panel

  • Jane T. Shuman is a nationally recognized authority on patient flow, practice efficiency, and ophthalmic technician training. Her experience spans roles as a scribe, technician, department leader, and private practice administrator. She directs the Allied Health Track at the Hawaiian Eye Meeting and serves as Secretary on the Board of Directors of Project Vision Hawaii. Jane regularly speaks at national and regional ophthalmology conferences, sharing practical strategies to improve practice operations and patient satisfaction.

    LinkedIn · Eyetechs

  • Scott LaBorwit, MD, is the former chief medical officer of Vision Innovation Partners and president and founder of Select Eye Care. He also serves as an associate professor of Ophthalmology at Johns Hopkins School of Medicine and on the Dean’s Advisory Council for the Carey School of Business at Johns Hopkins. His experience as a practicing ophthalmologist, healthcare entrepreneur, industry advisor, educator, and clinical leader gives him a perspective that bridges medicine, business, technology, and the realities of patient care.
