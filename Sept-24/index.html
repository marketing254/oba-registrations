About this session

Grow a dry-eye program that stays patient-centred

Join the Ophthalmology Business Academy for a focused discussion on how ophthalmology practices can build a financially sustainable dry-eye service line without compromising clinical judgment or patient trust. The panel will explore the decisions practices face when expanding cash-pay care, including how to keep recommendations clinically grounded, determine the right service model, and create an experience that helps patients understand both their condition and their treatment options.

This discussion will examine how practices can evaluate the clinical and business value of diagnostics such as meibography, osmolarity, and MMP-9, decide between a higher-volume care model and a more specialized service, and structure the patient journey across physicians, optometrists, technicians, and care coordinators. The goal is to identify practical approaches that support better clinical decisions, clearer patient communication, stronger team utilization, and a dry-eye program that can grow responsibly while remaining patient-centred.

This panel is for you if…

  • You own, lead, or manage an ophthalmology practice
  • You're launching or expanding cash-pay dry-eye services
  • You're unsure which diagnostics are worth the investment
  • You want a service model that grows without eroding patient trust
  • You want ODs, technicians, and coordinators to own more of the dry-eye journey
Panelist

Who's on the panel

  • Dr. Crystal Brimer is a Fellow of the AAO and primary clinical investigator who, after 18 years in practice, made a pivotal shift in 2017/2018 — designing the Oculus 5M software platform, converting her Wilmington, NC office into a thriving specialty dry eye referral center, and founding the Dry Eye Institute to equip doctors nationwide with the clinical knowledge and logistical tools to build their own dry eye practices, whether standalone or within an existing practice. Dr. Brimer is known for her passion to lecture and consult in the field of ocular surface disease, but she is equally driven by closing gaps in patient care — a mission that led her to found a nonprofit dedicated to helping patients access the out-of-pocket procedures they need.

The Ophthalmology Business Academy convenes practice owners, administrators, physicians, and industry experts for candid, non-promotional conversations about building stronger eye-care practices.
